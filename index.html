The Premise

A city severed from its nation

In 2092 a cabal seized the Presidential Palace in Bamako, and the Malian state came apart. Timbuktu was left on its own. Fifty years later, three powers fight over the old city of manuscripts. J-Sector's oligarchs sell power and water by the hour. Faya's zealots broadcast their dogma from mud-brick spires. The MLI-Z separatists hold the borderlands and want a country of their own.

Under the Sankoré precinct, the Kirina Resistance Front holds on to a different idea: free elections, restored rights, and a Timbuktu that belongs to Mali again. Its founder, Dr. Amadou, has just walked out into the night to find a power source that could set the city free. He has not come back.

"We will tear down their towers brick by brick until Mali is whole again."Khalil, son of Dr. Amadou

The title honours the Battle of Kirina (c. 1235), where Sundiata Keita's victory founded the Mali Empire. It is a story about unification, and so is this one.

Design Pillars

What makes Kirina different

Heritage you can touch, choices that cost something, and combat with real weight.

Heritage first

Banco before circuit

Sudano-Sahelian mud-brick architecture, Bogolan cloth and griot song come first, with technology woven quietly underneath. The Echo Layer lets players hack ruins and watch Timbuktu's golden-age libraries rebuild themselves in light.

Open world · branching

Every path is a choice

Start from one point and let your choices decide which factions you meet and how. The choices are exclusive. Take the data heist and the convoy is gone for good. Every choice moves your reputation and leads toward one of four endings.

Visceral combat

Stealth that turns loud

Firefights in first and third person, with heavy recoil, crumbling banco cover, doors that buckle and armor that dents. Khalil breaches, Awa weaves runes and snipes, and Dusé turns the enemy's turrets against them.

The Operatives

Two cousins, one missing father

Khalil and Awa are both playable. Switch between them freely in the open world.

Khalil

Desert scout · Breacher

Dr. Amadou's son, about thirty-two. He has his father's river-town calm and his mother's desert patience, and keeps his face behind his turban. A former KRF scout who still blames himself for letting his father go alone.

Awa

Tech-shaman · Ghost of the Sahel

Amadou's niece, about twenty-seven. Trained by griots and by the Front, she weaves Bogolan mud-cloth patterns into living code. With them she cracks energy spires, raises barriers and tags targets for the kill.

Dusé

Ally · Hacker of the Shadows

For years he has fought J-Sector from hidden relays, a voice and a violet glyph that nobody has met. In Act II he becomes the cousins' remote operator, hijacking turrets and rewriting the city's locks. Late in the story he finally walks in and shows his face. His name stays his own.

Dr. Amadou

The Missing Scholar

He was a soldier at twenty when the state fell. He became a historian, a cryptographer, and founder of the Kirina Resistance Front. He went looking for the legendary Kirina Core, left a letter in a Bogolan cipher for his son and his niece, and did not come back.

The World

Three flags over one city

Floating scholar bastions, sun-harvesting monuments and dunes that rearrange themselves. The landscape itself is a puzzle.

The Faya Sector · The Spires

The old scholarly heart, taken over by zealots of the "Codified Word". Transmission spires broadcast dogma day and night while the Wardens of the Spire march below. Music is banned, and so are manuscripts.

J-Sector · The Craft & Data Quarter

Factories, cyan data lines and the Generation Hall that powers the city. Chairman Lassana Vey and the Board of Nine rule through the Azure Syndicate, with its sentries, mechs, gunships and the blackout dampeners.

MLI-Z Borderlands · Free State of Tenzar

Fortified oasis farms and wind towers on the edge of the Erg of Echoes. Commandant Assalan Teyr's separatists have real grievances, and the player decides whether they become allies or enemies.

The Well of Sankoré · KRF

The resistance base hidden in the vaults beneath the Sankoré precinct. From the war table here, the fight for reunification is planned.

Beyond the Walls

The Bubble, a floating archive above the city. The Foggara, old water channels that became an undercity. The dunes of the Erg of Echoes. And Kirina Deep, where the Core is hidden.

Campaign · The Sahara Rebellion

From a missing father to a nation reunited

An open-world campaign in which your path decides which factions you meet, and in what order.

Prologue

The Vanishing

An empty vault, grid bypass keys and a letter in Bogolan cipher. The trail leads to the floating Bubble.

Act I

The Spires

Faya's despair. A disruptor at the main spire, and a duel with Marshal Kessar Vohl.

Act II

The Grid

Dusé joins. Choose one first strike. Then Operation Lantern: take a power core from J-Sector's stronghold.

Act III

The Winds

Ally with or fight Tenzar, race to Kirina Deep, and lay siege to the Azure Citadel to free Amadou.

Endings

Four Winds

Reunification, The Fragile Accord, The Iron Peace or The Silent Wind. Earned, never picked from a menu.

Strategy Gamma: Broadcast Tower Sabotage. If the alarms sound, sentries ring the ground and fighter jets fill the sky. A quiet stealth mission becomes a breakout under air bombardment.
Story mode

The Sahara Rebellion

A single-player open world across the city's sectors, the floating bastions, the Foggara undercity and the deep desert. You investigate through environmental hacking, deciphered ruins and informants, then fight it out in close-quarters battles.

Multiplayer

Harmattan Royale

Sixty players in squads of three drop from caravans into the Timbuktu region while a Harmattan sandstorm wall closes in. Recover Echo Relics, ride shifting dunes and survive Sun Stele overcharges.
