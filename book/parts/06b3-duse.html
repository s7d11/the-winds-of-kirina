Playing Dusé

For two acts he is the voice in your ear. After Interlude II, a few times, you are the voice, and someone else is talking to you.

Khalil and Awa carry the game. They are the two heroes the player swaps between in the open world, and nothing on this page changes that. In open-world play Dusé stays where he has always been: a violet ring on the HUD and a relay wheel, working through turrets, doors and lights from somewhere else. What changes after his face is revealed in "The Access Key" is that a handful of short, authored sequences put the player inside his boots. There are three kinds, and together they come to about an hour across the whole campaign.

Interlude II

The Kabara Roof

The end of "The Access Key": the meet with Director Halima Cissé, then holding the link outside her annex. About 20 minutes. The first time the player controls him.

The Siege

Ghost Run

Dusé alone on the Azure Citadel's relay floor while the cousins fight below. About 25 minutes, cross-cut with Khalil and Awa.

Optional

Relay Nights

Five side missions from the Well's Relay Room, 10 to 15 minutes each, rebuilding the network he lost when Cissé burned his relays.

Why play him at all

The reveal gives Dusé a body, and the sequences give that body weight. A man who has spent eleven years behind relays now has to stand on a roof where people can shoot him. Playing him also teaches the player what his remote help costs: every hijacked turret they called from the relay wheel in Act II was this, a man with a tablet, counting seconds.

How he differs from the cousins

Dusé is fragile. He has no plating and no heavy weapons, cannot pick up enemy guns, and has no melee kills. Two rifle hits put him down. He wins by making the room fight for him. The cousins solve a problem by going through it; Dusé solves it by owning what is already in the room.

The violet UI, from the inside

When the player becomes Dusé, the mud-cloth HUD gives way to his own. The Bogolan chevrons for health become three thin violet signal bars. The Grid bar becomes Tablet charge, which refills only while the tablet is lowered. The compass strip becomes a range ring that shows how far the nearest relay reaches. In Network View the world dims to slate and every camera, lock and turret is drawn as a violet glyph with its sightline traced in fine lines. Khalil and Awa show up as a cyan and a red marker carrying their current AI orders, and it is now their voices in the earpiece.

Sequence: The Kabara Roof

The end of "The Access Key". Night over the river port, a warehouse roof between the cranes, and a woman who has his name.

The questline's first two missions are played as Khalil or Awa. Control passes to Dusé at the foot of the last ladder, with no title card and no tutorial pop-up. The player simply finds themselves holding a tablet instead of a spear. Awa is on a crane jib a hundred metres off with her backpack rifle; Khalil waits in the stairwell below. Cissé is already on the roof with one Sentry Enforcer, two Hunter Drones and a skiff idling at the quay.

Beat by beat

  1. The climb. Raising the tablet teaches Network View with no prompt: drones, the Enforcer's command link and the crane floodlights light up violet. Awa's voice: "I see you. You climb like an accountant."
  2. The meet. Cissé makes her offer: amnesty in exchange for the KRF's Siege plans. The conversation is a dialogue wheel, and the tablet stays live throughout. While they talk, the player can quietly prepare the roof: loop a drone's feed, unlock the crane cab, slave a floodlight. Each prepared device is a free move once things go wrong. Prepare too much and Cissé notices the glyph on his screen, and the fight starts early.
  3. The refusal. Every branch ends with Dusé saying no. The Enforcer powers up. Dusé cannot hurt it, so the fight is about directing others: an override pulse freezes its targeting, and a tap on the tablet tells Awa where to put an anti-materiel round. This is the Neural Echo combo with the roles reversed.
  4. The skiff. Cissé runs for the quay. Stopping her means a 6-second hack on the skiff's drive while standing in the open. Succeed and the cousins take her alive. Fail, or decide the risk is not worth it, and she escapes into the dark.
  5. The annex link. The logs sit on an air-gapped server in Cissé's annex beside the Citadel, and the tablet link to Awa reaches only 40 m. Dusé lies flat on a billing-hall roof across the service yard while Awa goes in. For four minutes the player holds the link, rerouting cameras ahead of her, while a roof patrol comes up the fire stairs. Dusé can pulse it and slip past, or move and risk the link. If the link drops, Awa has to work on her own and the alert goes to Suspicious.
  6. The choice. Awa brings out the server and the questline's choice about the name follows, as set out in Part IV. If the confession branch is chosen, Dusé broadcasts it later that night from the Well's relay room.

Cissé does not raise her voice. She has the logs on her own tablet and holds it up so he can see his old name at the top of every commit.

Cissé "You wrote clean code. I've kept the scheduler running for eleven years and never had to patch it. That's the part I don't understand. A man who builds that well, living in a pirogue radio."

Dusé "It's a very good radio."

Cissé "The Siege plans, and your name never leaves this roof."

Dusé (his thumb moving on the tablet, out of her sight) "Director, you're standing under a crane I own."

Risk & consequence

If Cissé escapes the roof, she runs the Citadel's network defence in the Siege: Dusé's phase-4 override pulse lasts five seconds instead of ten, and she is waiting for him on the relay floor in Ghost Run. If Dusé goes down on the roof, there is no game over. Khalil drags him into the stairwell, Cissé escapes automatically, and Dusé's next line is an apology.

Sequence: Ghost Run

The Citadel's own network was never on the city grid. To take it, someone has to walk in and touch it.

The Ghost Signal at the outer wall comes from the Well, as remote as ever. It reaches every turret in the south of the city and stops at the Citadel's walls, because the tower runs on a sealed internal network. When the wall buckles, Nafi Kanta runs Dusé up the cooling-water channel in a pirogue with its engine cut. Ghost Run begins at the intake grate. If Dusé's trust is Withdrawn he has already gone silent, and the sequence does not exist: Awa takes the ducts blind and Khalil fights the Board Room without turned turrets.

Beat by beat

  1. The intake. Wet concrete, Hunter Drones, one guard post. Sidearm and pulse only. The relay floor sits on level 12, three floors under the Board Room and well above the prison block.
  2. The freight lift. Dusé overrides a lift and rides it up through floors in open combat. Each stop shows a different stage of Khalil's push up the garrison stairs.
  3. Lock 1: the ducts. On the relay floor Dusé opens the cooling-duct shutters. Hand-off: the player takes Awa for her descent toward the white room.
  4. Lock 2: the stairwell. Back as Dusé, he seizes the stairwell blast doors and two landing turrets. Hand-off: Khalil fights up through a garrison that is now taking fire from its own guns.
  5. The counter-hack. The floor's operators fight back. If Cissé escaped the roof, it is her: she locks doors around Dusé and sends a security team. He holds a server aisle with subverted turrets, his sidearm and the pulse. Every door he loses here is also locked on Khalil's stairs.
  6. Lock 3: the white room. Dusé holds the cell lock open while the player, as Awa, reaches Amadou. The rescue scene follows.
  7. The console. Dusé turns the two Board Room turrets for the Vey fight. Cissé, if present, surrenders at her console; he cages her in her own access booth and waits for an Unveiled squad. Control returns to Khalil for the Board Room.
Risk & consequence

If Dusé goes down, the sequence restarts from the last lock; he never dies here. Losses carry forward instead: every door lost in the counter-hack means one more Enforcer wave on Khalil's stairs, and failing the console leaves Vey's Board Room turrets in Syndicate hands. Cissé can be shot at her console, since the sidearm allows it. Doing so adds to the Iron tally, drops Dusé's trust to Withdrawn, and removes her testimony from Vey's trial in the Reunification epilogue.

Relay Nights and Ghost Signal in Person

Cissé burned his network in one night. He rebuilds it one lamp post at a time.

After Interlude II, Dusé spends some nights away from the Well. If his trust is at least Curious, a violet lamp on the Relay Room door marks a Relay Night: an optional mission played as Dusé, in person, in a district the cousins have already opened. Each one rebuilds a relay he lost or plants a new one. They can only be started at night, they never expire, and skipping them is never punished. In normal open-world play he goes back to the relay wheel the next morning.

Ghost Signal: remote and in person

Ghost Signal is described in full in the Ascension chapter. Most of the time it is remote: the cousins hand their Resonance to Dusé through the relay wheel, and he Ascends somewhere safe. After Kirina Deep he also carries a Seed of his own. In Ghost Run and in any Relay Night played after Act III's midpoint, he can Ascend on the spot, from a separate Resonance band that fills through turret subversions, non-lethal outcomes and staying below Searching.

Design note

The in-person form trades reach for depth. From the Well he can touch the whole city lightly. Standing in the room, he can take a single machine completely, and for fifteen seconds the most fragile person in the fight is the most dangerous one. Then the light goes out of his eyes and he is a man with an empty tablet again, which is the point.
